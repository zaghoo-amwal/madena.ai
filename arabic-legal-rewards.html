Better answers need better judges.

Arabic legal evaluation starts with a deceptively simple question: can we trust the reward?

Mohamed Zaghoo · Research note · November 2026

A model can produce a valid Arabic answer and still receive the wrong reward. Our Arabic Legal Reward Reliability study asks whether automated evaluators can distinguish a substantive legal error from a legitimate variation in wording.

When the checker gets it wrong

Earlier checks sometimes confused rejecting an action with requesting it, or a contractual example with a statement of statutory law. A combined revision of checker logic and trap definitions reduced errors on a small held-out set.

The experiment used 67 AI-authored sentences: 37 for development and 30 held out. The result applies to the combined changes; it does not isolate the effect of any one technique.

Expert criteria are only half the work

A separate expert review revised two legal rubrics. Weak prompt-copy controls received lower rewards, but stronger penalties also amplified existing detection errors and an unstated jurisdiction requirement.

The lesson: a more consequential criterion needs a more reliable check. A rubric can reflect professional priorities while its implementation still rewards or penalises the wrong thing.

Useful training rewards need both expert judgment and reliable verification. More examples cannot fix an ambiguous task or a faulty answer key.

What comes next

Independent expert grading, broader Arabic phrasing, and new scenario families should test whether these checks generalise. Improving trained-model behavior remains a future experiment.

Scope and limitations

The public verifier experiment is small and uses AI-authored labels, not independent legal judgments. Zero errors in 17 compliant sentences does not establish error-free performance. The expert reviewed rubrics, not model answers; the private rubric-rescoring results are not fully reproducible from the public release. No model weights were trained or updated.

Source: Arabic Legal Reward Reliability: experiments, methods, and public demo.
