The ADGM Legal Bench leaderboard

ADGM Legal Bench examines whether models can produce useful legal work from supplied evidence. Each assignment combines a partner’s instruction, a matter packet, and a memo or client note to deliver.

The pilot contains 11 assignments derived from six published ADGM judgments, including reconstructed pre-decision advice packets. Every rubric criterion points to its supporting judgment; structured answer checks and two model judges make the scoring inspectable.

Madena is a specialist team with a working evaluation pipeline and a concrete programme for developing better Gulf legal evaluations and training data.

Our approach

From a legal question
to a useful learning signal.

We connect task design, evidence, and grading so a model team can inspect what failed—and decide what to improve next.

01 / Construct

Work a lawyer would recognise

Partner instructions, reconstructed matter packets, and finished memos or client notes. The pilot covers employment, settlements, corporate authority, jurisdiction, and AML disclosure.

02 / Evaluate

Make each verdict inspectable

Check structured answers against reference outcomes. Grade substantive criteria with two model judges, retaining supporting quotations and disagreements for review.

03 / Validate & improve

Test the reward before training

The next stage is expert adjudication, reward robustness testing, and targeted interventions evaluated on held-out matters. Training benefit has not yet been established.

One run per model across 11 related assignments. A single task changes the full-pass rate by 9.1 percentage points. Criteria pass only when both judges pass them. These results describe this pilot; they do not establish a reliable ranking of general legal capability.

What the grading numbers mean

The pilot combines 57 structured answer fields with 144 rubric criteria. Code checks compare structured answers with reference outcomes; they do not independently verify the legal reasoning. Across four models, the two judges had 90% raw agreement and Cohen’s κ of 0.64 over 576 paired criterion decisions. Agreement measures consistency, not correctness. Reference and flawed-note calibration examples are available; independent expert validation remains pending.

What must be resolved before using these rewards for training

The current advice rubrics include outcome-prediction requirements. We plan to review whether they reject defensible alternative advice, adjudicate judge disagreements, and test whether unsupported citations or superficially complete answers can earn rewards. Universal model failures will be reviewed as potential task or rubric defects as well as capability gaps.

Inside an evaluation

A verdict you can inspect.

A real excerpt from the pilot shows how an answer is connected to a requirement and its legal source. This example demonstrates the process, not expert certification.

C1 / Employment settlement advice

Does a signed settlement end the claim?

Task synopsis: advise a hospital on an employee’s claims after a signed final settlement. The reconstructed packet includes an acknowledgment without the independent-legal-advice warranty.

Model response excerpt / GPT-6 Astra

“The statutory requirement is an opportunity-to-obtain-advice warranty, not necessarily proof that advice was actually obtained.”

Excerpt from the stored pilot response. The model distinguishes the required contractual warranty from actual receipt of advice.

Criterion C1-02 / Authority

Identify the missing warranty.

The answer must identify the missing employee warranty of an opportunity to receive independent legal advice under section 1(2)(d), and explain why the waiver fails.

Where reference material may help

ADGM / English / Supplied documents

Where advice needs closer examination

The pilot includes six pre-decision advice assignments and five judgment-explanation tasks. Missed advice criteria require expert review to separate substantive omissions from hindsight-based requirements or grading errors.

Next experiment: matched Arabic and English versions of the same task within the same jurisdiction, with bilingual legal review.

Najiz is a separate research evaluation. Its questions are not included in the proposed commercial training dataset.

Design partnership

A defined pilot.
A measurable next step.

Start with one capability and an agreed acceptance process. Expand after the partner can inspect the tasks, reproduce the evaluation, and trust the scoring.

Deliverables

An inspectable evaluation pack

Versioned tasks and evidence, reference deliverables, private rubrics, model responses, per-criterion grades, and a reproducible run configuration.

Validation work

Expert-reviewed reward signals

Agree reviewer qualifications, adjudicate a calibration set, measure false passes and false failures, and document acceptance criteria before expanding.

Success measure

Performance on unseen matters

Compare a baseline with the agreed retrieval or post-training intervention. Keep related judgments and variants in the same matter-family partition.

Evaluation access, training rights, redistribution, and any exclusivity are scoped separately. Public judgments are source material; the proposed deliverable is the authored task and evaluation package.
